Amazon Review Analytics

What can review text tell a seller that a star rating cannot? Our team built a reproducible text-analytics workflow to compare sentiment, identify complaint themes, and summarize product-level signals.

300REVIEWS ANALYZED
10PRODUCTS IN THE SAMPLE
48.67%TEXT / STAR-LABEL AGREEMENT

THE WORKFLOW

Drew a balanced, stratified 300-review sample from the 568,454-review Amazon Fine Food Reviews dataset. Applied rating-derived comparison labels, VADER sentiment scoring, tokenization, stop-word removal, keyword-based complaint classification, and product-level aggregation.

THE USEFUL FINDINGS

  • Complaint themes: taste appeared in 39 of the 100 negative sample reviews, followed by packaging in 18 and quality in 10.
  • Evaluation mattered: overall agreement with star-derived labels was 48.67%, including 93% for positive reviews and just 5% for neutral reviews.
  • The practical lesson: mixed language and numeric ratings do not always tell the same story. Reviewing failures makes the output more useful than presenting a score alone.

PROJECT CONTEXT

Academic team project by Afnan Rafid, Christy Johnson, Tiffany Nguyen, Grecia Lopez Gonzalez, and Muataz Mahdi for Analytics Programming at the University of Oklahoma. The portfolio includes Python scripts, notebooks, sample data, charts, and documented results.

Scope & limitations

The deliberately balanced sample does not represent the natural sentiment distribution of all Amazon reviews. Star ratings are imperfect comparison labels; the agreement rate is not independently validated model accuracy.
