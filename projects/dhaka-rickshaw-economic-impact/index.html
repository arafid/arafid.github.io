Dhaka Rickshaw Economic Impact Study

A field study built from interviews with 90 rickshaw pullers across three areas of Dhaka, examining how COVID-19 affected income, expenses, health, and day-to-day livelihoods.

90INTERVIEWS
3DHAKA AREAS
6 mo.INCOME / EXPENSE SERIES

THE FIELD STUDY

I interviewed 90 rickshaw pullers—30 each in Dhanmondi, Malibagh, and Mirpur—using a questionnaire covering work patterns, income, expenses, health, COVID-19 awareness, PPE use, borrowing, and perceived changes during the pandemic. The interviews were conducted in March 2021 and formed the primary data for the paper.

FROM INTERVIEWS TO DATA

The study summarized before/after income and expense ranges and constructed a six-month series of average monthly income and expenses from October 2020 through March 2021. The original paper used those monthly values to examine the relationship between income and spending.

THE REGRESSION WORK

The original analysis modeled monthly expenses as a function of monthly income and reported an R² of approximately 0.98, with the fitted equation Expenses = 0.71 × Income − 795.867. The useful takeaway is the direction of the relationship in this small historical series: as reported income fell, reported spending also fell.

Analytical takeaway

The model captured a strong positive relationship in this small series: lower reported income was associated with lower reported spending. The useful skill is not just fitting the line, but qualifying what the line can and cannot support.

This is not a causal or predictive model. The regression uses only six monthly aggregate observations, and the underlying financial values were self-reported.

WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUGGESTED

  • Income pressure: the interviewed workers reported lower daily and monthly income during the pandemic.
  • Spending pressure: lower income was accompanied by lower reported expenses, including spending tied to food, medicine, rent, and other household needs.
  • Broader livelihood strain: the paper connected reduced passenger demand with physical strain, mental stress, and greater vulnerability to a poverty trap.

LIMITATIONS

The financial figures were self-reported estimates rather than transaction records. The sample came from three high-rickshaw-activity areas rather than a probability sample of Dhaka, and the regression used only six monthly aggregate observations. The high R² should therefore be read as a descriptive result from this historical paper—not as predictive validation or proof of causality.

Project context

Completed as a 4,000-word IB Economics Extended Essay during the COVID-19 pandemic, this study combined original field interviews with descriptive and regression analysis. The repository includes the original research paper alongside methodology notes that clarify the study’s sampling, measurement, and analytical limitations.
