La Cocina de la Abuela Mobile App Planning

An end-to-end project-management case for a proposed mobile application, moving from business requirements and critical-path planning through cost, risk, quality, stakeholder communication, change control, and closeout.

THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

The case centered on a taco truck whose ordering and customer communication were largely manual. The proposed mobile solution aimed to support ordering and checkout, loyalty tracking, notifications and promotions, employee scheduling and time entry, and management visibility into sales and operations.

FROM REQUIREMENTS TO A PROJECT PLAN

The team built the work across three milestones. The first established scope, requirements, phases, task dependencies, a Gantt-style schedule, a critical path, and initial app mockups. The second added cost and procurement planning, a risk register, measurable quality criteria, testing approaches, accessibility and payment-security considerations, and updated mockups. The final milestone added stakeholder analysis, a communication register, change control, final screen concepts, and a project closeout plan.

RISK, QUALITY, AND CONTROL THINKING

  • Risk planning: the register covered scope creep, app-store rejection, third-party downtime, budget overruns, compatibility, data breach, staffing, adoption, testing delays, and compliance.
  • Quality targets: the plan used criteria such as sub-two-second load time, a crash-free-rate target above 99.9%, an 85% usability target, accessibility alignment, and secure payment handling.
  • Testing approach: the team planned unit, integration, regression, user-acceptance, accessibility, performance, cross-platform, and automated testing.

STAKEHOLDERS AND COMMUNICATION

The stakeholder plan distinguished customers, employees, and management by their needs, influence, and power. The communication register then mapped audiences to purpose, cadence, and channel—for example daily standups, weekly status updates, milestone reviews, UAT feedback, and sponsor communication using tools such as Slack or Teams, email, Asana, video meetings, and Google Forms.

MY DOCUMENTED LEARNING

My individual reflection focused especially on communication management and procurement. I wrote about matching different stakeholder groups to appropriate communication channels and using participation and feedback to judge whether communication was working. I also highlighted procurement concepts such as contract types and make-or-buy analysis.

WHY IT BELONGS IN THE PORTFOLIO

This project shows a different side of technology work than coding or analytics: translating a business need into requirements, sequencing the work, planning resources and vendors, identifying risk, defining quality, managing stakeholders, controlling changes, and thinking about handoff. It is included as supporting work rather than a flagship technical build.

Scope & limitations

This was an academic team planning and prototyping exercise for MIS 4433: Project Management at the University of Oklahoma. The mobile application was not deployed as a production system. Budgets, schedules, risk scores, quality targets, change events, and closeout outcomes in the original class documents were planning/scenario artifacts rather than audited real-world results. Team deliverables are presented as team work; this portfolio does not claim sole authorship.
