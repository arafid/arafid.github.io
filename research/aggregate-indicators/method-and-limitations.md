# Method and limitations

## What the paper demonstrates
The investigation compared alternative shapes against grouped household income data and questioned whether an aggregate statistic alone adequately describes household circumstances. It examined the tradeoff between polynomial fit, local fit, and interpretable piecewise functions.

## Retrospective qualifications
1. GDP measures production; it is not a direct measure of household income or well-being. Differences between these measures alone do not establish the cause of inequality.
2. Household observations must not be described as individual incomes. Household size and composition matter.
3. Appendix A is a partial table. Its full inflation/dollar-basis and methodology footnotes are not reproduced in the paper. Before making real-income changes across years, verify the original table's price basis and survey comparability. Do not adjust already constant-dollar data for inflation a second time.
4. The plotted bracket widths differ. A probability-density histogram requires dividing each bracket's probability mass by its width. A continuous model also needs non-negativity and an integral of one over its stated domain. The original paper explicitly notes normalization was unresolved.
5. A density f(x) at a point is not the cumulative probability up to that point. Interval probabilities require integrating the density; the original introductory wording should not be treated as a precise definition.
6. The quadratic/exponential cutoff was chosen from the observed shape. It was not validated out of sample, and smoothness, normalization, and parameter uncertainty were not established.
7. The paper's broad poverty, purchasing-power, and causal interpretations go beyond what these grouped data and regressions alone establish. The portfolio therefore presents the modeling process and limitations, without repeating those claims as verified results.
8. No chi-square statistic is presented here: the available source paper does not support a reproducible goodness-of-fit calculation with expected counts and fitted-parameter degrees of freedom. No numerical model-accuracy claim is made.
